# 2009 Kigurumi Chronicle

> The 2010-2025 volumes are complete. Following the rule that a completed year is not rebuilt and only one earlier year is prepared per run, this volume adds 2009 only. The verifiable primary material is concentrated in Tokyo Game Show 2009's organizer archive; specialist gatherings without reliable dates, places, and outcomes are not reconstructed from later evidence.

## 1. Evidence boundary

Tokyo Game Show 2009's official overview lists September 24-25 as business days and September 26-27 as public days at Makuhari Messe. This volume uses the organizer's overview, visitor rules, and September 27 closing report to distinguish published rules from the exhibition as confirmed after closing.[^tgs-overview][^tgs-rules][^tgs-report]

Tokyo Game Show is a general game exhibition, not an animegao kigurumi event. Its rules directly mention “large kigurumi,” but identify no animegao participant, count, program, or attendance result. This volume records **the explicit public-exhibition boundary for large kigurumi and the adjacent participation workflow**. It does not turn general cosplay rules or total exhibition attendance into evidence of kigurumi attendance.

## 2. Verifiable nodes

### September 26-27: the public-day baggage-screening plan included cosplay participants

The organizer said it would conduct baggage screening on both public days and warned cosplay participants carrying more belongings to allow additional entry time. Prohibited items included cosplay umbrellas, canes, rackets, and similar props longer than 50 centimetres, as well as sharp or dangerous objects that could injure others.[^tgs-rules]

This establishes that the organizer placed cosplay luggage and props inside its published safety plan. It is not a screening-completion report, does not show that an animegao or large-kigurumi participant was actually screened, and is not evidence of a specialist gathering.

### September 26-27: the rules explicitly restricted large kigurumi

The costume rules grouped large kigurumi, large papier-mache forms, and floor-dragging skirts or mantles among costumes that could inconvenience surrounding visitors. A violation could result in the participant being asked to stop cosplaying. The wording concerns size, circulation, and public safety; it is not a synonym for animegao, official character suits, or every form of kigurumi.[^tgs-rules]

The organizer's closing report confirms that Tokyo Game Show 2009 ran from September 24 through 27 and closed, but does not separately report cosplay programs or large-kigurumi participation. The existence of a rule must therefore remain separate from evidence that anyone attended in that form.[^tgs-report]

### September 26-27: changing, cloakroom, and photo consent formed a public workflow

The designated changing room cost JPY 500 including cloakroom use. Changing elsewhere and eating, resting, or photographing inside the room were prohibited; costumes were checked at the exit, arrival or departure in costume was prohibited, and sewing kits were available from staff. Changing-room registration had to be completed by 16:00.[^tgs-rules]

Photography was limited to part of Yasuragi Mall between Halls 3 and 4. Photographers had to obtain the subject's permission and use a wall background to exclude bystanders. Commercial photography, submission to publications, and sale of venue photographs were prohibited; personal-site publication also required prior disclosure and permission.[^tgs-rules]

## 5. Annual conclusion

The stable 2009 record is that Tokyo Game Show placed large kigurumi, cosplay baggage, designated changing, cloakroom use, and photo consent inside its public-exhibition rules. The closing report confirms the exhibition as a whole, but does not prove attendance by animegao or large-kigurumi participants. The 2010 volume therefore retains only material changes: the new integrated cosplay initiative, Hall 7 early changing, and the Dance Night extension.
