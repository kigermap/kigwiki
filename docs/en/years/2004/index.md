# 2004 Kigurumi Chronicle

> The years 2005-2025 are complete. Following the rule to avoid reworking completed years and add only one earlier year per run, this volume adds 2004 only. Tokyo Game Show 2004's organizer archive directly names `大きな着ぐるみ` (large kigurumi) and preserves a complete changing, cloakroom, and photography workflow. This volume moves those shared provisions to the earliest currently verifiable node instead of treating 2006 as their starting point.

## 1. Scope and evidence boundary

Tokyo Game Show was a general game exhibition, not a specialist animegao kigurumi event. The Japanese rule's `大きな着ぐるみ` means **large** kigurumi that could inconvenience nearby visitors and appears beside large papier-mache forms and floor-dragging skirts or capes. It is a size, circulation, and public-safety category, not a synonym for animegao, bishoujo kigurumi, or every masked costume.[^tgs-rules]

The rule proves only that the organizer governed this costume class in advance; it does not prove that anyone attended in it. The closing report's 160,096 figure is total attendance for the game exhibition and cannot be converted into a cosplay, kigurumi, or animegao participant count.[^tgs-report]

## 2. Verifiable nodes

### September 24-26: calendar, venue, and public-day window

The organizer's home page lists September 24 as Business Day, September 25-26 as public days, Makuhari Messe as the venue, and 10:00-17:00 as opening hours. It also provides a separate rules entry for cosplayers.[^tgs-home]

### September 25-26: large kigurumi enter the public costume boundary

The rules group large kigurumi, large papier-mache forms, and floor-dragging skirts or capes as costumes that inconvenience others; a complaint could also lead the organizer to stop the cosplay. Props had a separate 50-centimeter limit, with dangerous weapons, combustibles, strong odors, and noise-producing equipment or conduct prohibited.[^tgs-rules]

This is the earliest directly verifiable public rule for large kigurumi among the years now catalogued, but it remains a policy record rather than evidence of attendance.

### September 25-26: changing, storage, inspection, and photo consent form a complete workflow

The designated changing room cost JPY 500 including cloakroom use. Changing elsewhere, including toilets, and arriving or leaving in costume were prohibited. Eating, resting, and photography were barred inside the changing room; staff checked costumes at its exit and could hold non-compliant props.[^tgs-rules]

Photography was limited to part of the Yasuragi Mall between Halls 3 and 4. Photographers had to obtain the subject's permission and use a wall as the background to avoid including unrelated people. Sale or submission of venue photos to publications was prohibited, and personal-homepage publication also required prior explanation and permission.[^tgs-rules]

### September 27: the organizer confirms completion of the three-day show

The closing report confirms that the show ran September 24-26 and recorded 160,096 admissions across the three days.[^tgs-report] It establishes that the overall event corresponding to the rules took place, but it gives no separate account of cosplay-rule enforcement and identifies no large-kigurumi or animegao participant.

## 5. Annual conclusion

The stable 2004 record is that Tokyo Game Show placed large kigurumi, designated changing, cloakroom storage, costume inspection, and photo consent inside its published cosplay workflow, while a closing report confirms the overall show was completed. The public archive still does not prove that animegao or large kigurumi attended. Provisions repeated item-for-item in 2006 now use 2004 as their baseline, with later volumes keeping only continuity or change. The next unprocessed year is 2003.
