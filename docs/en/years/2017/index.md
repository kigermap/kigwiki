# 2017 Kigurumi Chronicle

> The 2018-2025 volumes are complete. Following the one-year-backward rule, this edition covers 2017 only. Verifiable material is concentrated in one community-run survey and the public cosplay rules of a general anime convention. Undated specialist gatherings are not converted into facts.

## 1. Evidence boundary

This volume uses two types of record. AnimeJapan's first-party pages establish dates, venue, and event rules. The archived *Kigurumi Census Results Report* establishes the survey period, response count, questions, and the author's own limitations. It was neither a random sample nor peer-reviewed research, so its percentages cannot represent or be extrapolated to the whole kigurumi community.

The annual page does not reproduce the report's 73 pages and 205 figures. It records the report's chronological significance and links to the [complete archived report](../../sources/survey-reports/kigurumi-census-2017/index.md).

## 2. Verified nodes

### March 25-26: AnimeJapan 2017 defines public-space boundaries for kigurumi

AnimeJapan 2017's public days ran March 25-26 in Tokyo Big Sight East Halls 1-7. Cosplayers World provided paid registration, changing rooms, cloakrooms, and indoor and outdoor photography areas.[^aj-outline][^aj-cosplay]

The rules confined costumes that restricted vision, including face coverings, masks, and eye patches, and hard-to-move forms such as large constructions, kigurumi, or trailing costumes to the cosplay areas. They also required a subject's permission before photography, prohibited forcing poses, and prohibited photography in changing rooms.[^aj-cosplay] This is not evidence of a specialist animegao event. It is a public-governance record that directly names kigurumi and addresses movement risk, privacy, and consent.

### About one week from October 4: a community census creates a quantitative snapshot

The “Kigurumi Census” initiated by Kussy (@kussy_tessy) received 221 responses. After excluding one response that did not accept the opening consent statement, 220 remained valid. The questionnaire circulated through the author's surrounding Twitter network. Its preface explicitly acknowledges population bias, question framing, and lack of neutrality, so this archive treats it as a **bounded community self-record**, not a population census or academic finding.[^census]

The report covers participation, years active, masks and clothing, production sources, photography, social contact, identity, and intimate subjects. Two limited indicators are useful for the annual overview: among the 217 valid answers to the participation-mode question, 53% said they wore kigurumi at events or offline gatherings and 15% mainly wore it alone; the experience question had 216 valid answers and an average of 5.9 years, while after non-participants were excluded, more than half still had no more than five years of activity.[^census] These percentages describe this sample only.

## 5. Annual conclusion

The strongest 2017 record is not a dense event list but two kinds of infrastructure. A large public venue had explicit rules for kigurumi visibility, movement, photography, and privacy, while the community produced a comparatively large quantitative self-record. Both require their boundaries: a general-convention rule is not a specialist-event history, and a convenience sample is not the whole community.
