# 2013 Kigurumi Chronicle

> The 2014-2025 volumes are complete. Following the rule to avoid completed years and move backward one year at a time, this edition covers 2013 only. Accessible first-party material is concentrated in Tokyo Game Show 2013's organizer rules. Specialist gatherings without a reliable date, place, and outcome are not converted into facts.

## 1. Evidence boundary

This volume uses the Japanese and English cosplay guidance retained in Tokyo Game Show 2013's official archive. The pages establish the event dates and venue and the costume, changing, movement, photography, and image-publication conditions announced by the organizer.[^tgs-rules]

Tokyo Game Show is a general game exhibition, not a specialist animegao kigurumi event. The Japanese rules directly refer to “large kigurumi” (`大きな着ぐるみ`), but identify no animegao participant, specialist gathering, or participant count. This volume therefore records **how a public convention governed large kigurumi and adjacent cosplay infrastructure**. Ordinary cosplay, total attendance, and the existence of a rule are not treated as evidence that animegao participants attended.

## 2. Verified nodes

### September 19-22: large kigurumi enter a public convention's costume boundary

The Tokyo Game Show 2013 archive lists September 19-20 as Business Days and September 21-22 as Public Days at Makuhari Messe. Its cosplayer rules classify large kigurumi, large papier-mache forms, and floor-dragging skirts or mantles as costumes that could obstruct others. Skirts and mantles could be extended for photography only and had to be folded while moving. The English edition renders the relevant category as “large size full-body suits.”[^tgs-rules]

This did not ban every kigurumi and does not identify what mask characters appeared. The narrower verified fact is that, by 2013, a major public game exhibition explicitly treated large kigurumi as a costume-governance issue involving circulation space and effects on nearby visitors.

### September 21-22: changing, designated photography, and publication consent share one participation framework

The same page limits changing to the cosplay changing rooms in Hall 9, forbids changing in restrooms or elsewhere, and prohibits eating, resting, and photography in the changing rooms. It also announces early changing from the first train at Kaihin-Makuhari Station until 08:00, cloakroom conditions, and a ban on arriving or leaving in costume.[^tgs-rules]

Photography was limited to designated areas inside and outside Hall 9 and in a specified parking area. Photographers had to obtain permission from the subject, and publishing an image on a personal website required the relevant person's approval. The organizer archive proves that these conditions were published. It is not a post-event report and cannot by itself prove that every facility operated as planned or that a kigurumi participant used it.

## 5. Annual conclusion

The stable 2013 record is not a specialist animegao event list but a public-convention boundary. Tokyo Game Show placed large kigurumi within circulation and space management while publishing changing-room, cloakroom, designated-photography, photography-permission, and image-publication conditions. These rules show that kigurumi had entered the governance vocabulary of a major public cosplay setting. They do not prove named participants, a specialist gathering, actual facility use, or community scale; those gaps remain open.
